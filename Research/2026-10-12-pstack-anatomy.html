Your mental model

Right on three counts, with two additions

correct

poteto-mode is the entry and the router

The SessionStart hook tells the agent to invoke it for multi-file work, design choices, and bugs or perf issues with an unknown cause. It then routes to a playbook.

correct

A playbook is a recipe

The agent copies the steps into its todolist word for word. Skipping a step needs a written skip: <reason>. Every playbook ends by running Opening a PR.

correct

Principles shape the steps

Playbook steps name principles inline. For example, Feature step 3 names separate-before-serializing-shared-state.

addition

Principles also fire on their own

poteto-mode lists all 24 principles, each with its own "when" clause. One applies whenever its situation comes up, in any playbook. The agent must read a principle's leaf SKILL.md before citing it.

addition

A separate trigger layer

poteto-mode's Non-negotiables fire workflow skills by situation, whatever the playbook. Any prose runs unslop, every commit runs deslop, every review runs no-comments. A "which approach?" question runs the Prototype playbook first.

Design moves

What keeps the crossing manageable

The index holds the triggers; each leaf holds the content

poteto-mode keeps one line per principle: when it applies. The full text lives only in the leaf skill, and the agent must read it before citing it. Context stays small until a principle is actually needed.

Recipes are copied, not paraphrased

Playbook steps go into the todolist word for word. A skipped step stays in the list with its reason, so drift is visible.

Roles are separate from models

Skills name a role slot, never a model. One line in the override sheet changes which model fills a role in every skill.

Extensions quote the steps they change

A project playbook anchors each change on the quoted text of a built-in step. check-playbooks.mjs fails when an upgrade rewrites that text.
